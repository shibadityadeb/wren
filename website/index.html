Wren helps turn the things you mean to do into things you can actually follow through on. Capture a thought, keep track of a commitment, ask a connected tool to do a piece of work, and return when it is finished.

01 / Capture, naturally

One place to capture what matters.

Start with a sentence, not a form. Give Wren a task, a commitment, a deadline or a reminder in ordinary language, then review what needs doing.

A global shortcut opens a small capture panel over whatever you're doing. Wren saves your exact words first, reads any date in them, and turns “I need to finish this report by Friday” into a task called Finish this report, due Friday. Moving, snoozing and completing work the same way: “move the research task to Sunday”, with a preview you confirm.

Available in the development build  Works without any account, and without AI. An optional local model on your Mac can improve how sentences are read.

02 / Speak, naturally

Just say what needs doing.

Wren is being built around a simple idea: your personal assistant should fit into the way you already work. A quick voice command or keyboard shortcut should be enough to capture a thought, create a reminder or start a supported action.

  1. Hold ⌥Space anywhere: Wren says it's listening
  2. Speak while you hold the keys
  3. Your words appear as they're recognised
  4. Let go: the microphone turns off and Wren transcribes on your Mac
  5. Wren runs it through the same path as typing, and shows the result

In development

The voice pipeline is built: Wren uses Apple's on-device speech recognition and refuses to run if on-device recognition isn't available, and no audio is stored. It hasn't yet been verified end to end with a real microphone. Wren never listens in the background: the microphone opens only when you press the shortcut or click Voice, and closes when your command ends. Voice works only while Wren is running and you've allowed Microphone and Speech Recognition.

03 / Give the work a place to go

Ask GPT. Get on with your day.

Sometimes you do not need another assistant explaining everything to you. You need a tool to do a piece of work, finish it, and let you know when it is ready.

Say or type “Ask GPT to research the fundamentals of quantum physics.” Wren opens it on the ChatGPT website, in your own account, so it becomes a normal ChatGPT conversation you can continue and find in your history. Link that conversation in Wren and you're back in it with one click. Prefer the answer inside Wren? Switch to running it with your connected ChatGPT plan, and Wren tells you when it's done.

  • No API key and no separate billing: the website is your own ChatGPT, and the in-Wren option uses your plan through Sign in with ChatGPT.
  • Only your request is sent. Nothing else from Wren is attached unless you add it yourself.
  • Honest status. Wren doesn't remote-control the ChatGPT website, so a website request shows “awaiting your submission”, never “done”. Results run in Wren stay in Wren, and Wren never claims they're ChatGPT conversations.

Requires account authorization  A real request on a ChatGPT plan has been run through this pipeline in the development build. It needs a ChatGPT plan that allows app sign-in.

04 / Attention, not alerts

Your inbox, without the noise.

Your inbox holds more than messages. It holds decisions, opportunities, deadlines and things worth remembering. Wren is being built to help you notice what deserves attention and leave the rest alone.

The design: a short morning email digest, a quiet alert only when something genuinely urgent arrives, and a reason next to everything Wren highlights, which you can correct. Spam and Trash are never looked at, and email is sorted on your Mac by rules, never sent to an AI service.

  1. Reading messagesHeaders, labels and Gmail's short preview. Read-only permission.

05 / A thought becomes a next step

Remember it now.
Let Wren remind you later.

Reminders are handed to macOS's own notification system when you set them, so a reminder that was scheduled successfully can still arrive after you've quit Wren, as long as notifications are allowed and macOS delivers them. Completing, snoozing or moving it updates what's scheduled, without duplicates.

In development  Scheduling is built and tested. A final live check of delivery after quitting is still to be done. A quit Wren doesn't listen for “Hey Wren” or check email.

  2. SavedIn Wren, with its time and status.

A different kind of assistant

Useful technology should feel like less technology.

  1. i.

    Capture without friction

    Express what needs doing in ordinary language. Wren saves your words first, and nothing you type is lost because something else failed.

  2. ii.

    Local-first foundations

    Your items, reminders, goals and history live on your Mac. The core of Wren works without an account or an internet connection.

  3. iii.

    Connections by choice

    External accounts are connected deliberately, and what Wren can do with each one depends on the permissions you grant. Disconnecting is one click.

  4. iv.

    Actions, not just answers

    Wren is built to help you follow through: set the reminder, hand off the piece of work, and bring you back to the result.

  5. v.

    Quiet by design

    Fewer interruptions, with the important commitments still in view. An alert should earn its place.

Why Wren?

Small bird. Quiet presence. Thoughtful purpose.

A wren is a small bird. The name felt right for a companion that should be useful without becoming the centre of attention: alert to what matters, close at hand when needed, and otherwise happy to let you get on with your day.

Wren is not meant to take over your work or your decisions. It is meant to help you keep hold of what matters, make room for the next step, and return your attention to the things you care about.

Independently built

A personal project.
A thoughtful purpose.

Wren is an independent project, designed and built by Shibaditya Deb.

Wren is being built around a simple ambition: create a useful everyday Mac companion that can help people capture commitments, delegate work and keep important things from being forgotten, without demanding constant attention in return.

The project is evolving in the open. Its design, implementation and roadmap continue to grow as the product becomes more useful.

FreeDuring the preview

Wren is currently free to use during its preview. There is no Wren subscription, no in-app purchase, and no charge from Wren for the features available in the current preview.

Connected providers have their own permissions, eligibility requirements, quotas and terms. Wren's ChatGPT connection uses your own ChatGPT account and plan rather than an API key you'd pay for separately; requests count toward your plan's usage, and the plan must allow apps to use it.

  • No Wren subscription during the current preview.
  • No Wren in-app purchase.
  • No paid feature tiers or checkout flow.
  • Third-party accounts and services remain subject to their own requirements.
  • Future pricing, if any, will be communicated transparently.

Privacy

Your Mac. Your data. Your control.

Privacy is a product decision, not a line in the footer. Wren is designed around local-first foundations and explicit connections to external services. You should understand what stays on your Mac, what leaves it, and why.

Read the Privacy Policy →

Stays on your Mac

  • Your items, reminders, goals, history and settings, in Wren's folder in your user Library.
  • Voice: Wren uses Apple's on-device recognition and doesn't store audio.
  • Email triage runs on your Mac, by rules.
  • The optional local AI model runs on your Mac through Ollama.

Leaves only when you ask

  • A request you send to GPT goes to OpenAI with your account, with only the text you chose.
  • Connected accounts are read with the permissions you granted, when a feature needs them.
  • Email content is never sent to an AI service.
  • No analytics or tracking in the app or on this website.

Yours to inspect and remove

  • Export your items and history, and make or restore backups.
  • Disconnecting an account revokes Wren's access and deletes its sign-in and cached mail.
  • “Remove all Wren data” deletes everything and signs out of connected accounts.
  • Sign-in tokens are private files in Wren's folder, readable only by your macOS user, not the Keychain.
